Chedi Wat San Nhong

The ancient monument of Wat San Nhong stands on the southern edge of "Thung Chedi" (Chedi Field), an area within the old city of Hot, in Hot Sub-district, Hot District, Chiang Mai Province. The chedi faces east toward the Ping River. No definitive record of the construction survives, but its artistic style and archaeological evidence place it within roughly the 15th to 17th centuries CE.

Chedi Wat San Nhong is a bell-shaped chedi in the Lanna art style, set on a square plan measuring 10.2 by 10.2 metres. It stands on a base raised about 30 centimetres above ground level, which joins the vihara and the principal chedi onto a single shared platform, forming a circumambulatory terrace (Pradakshina platform). On the western side, this base extends to meet the temple's boundary wall.

Structural Elements
  1. The Base, is the lowest tier is a stepped plinth (Than Khiang) on a square plan, with lotus-bud pillars (Sao Hua Bua) decorating each corner. On every side of the plinth stand altar platforms formed of lotus-moulded bases adorned with bead-and-reel moulding. The eastern altar platform is the largest and stands apart from the plinth, distinct from the other three sides, it displays finely detailed stucco work in both floral motifs and animal figures, including peacocks, elephants, Singhas (lions), and Qilins (mythical hybrid creatures). Above this rise indented-corner plinths, stacked in three receding tiers.
  2. The Body (Relic chamber), is set on an indented-corner square plan 6.5 metres wide, decorated with bead-and-reel moulding and stucco ornament in the form of framed niches with cloud-like scrollwork.
  3. The Upper Part, consists of circular-plan stepped plinths in three receding tiers, supporting the bell-shaped body and a square-plan throne. The uppermost section has broken away and is now lost. Archaeological excavation at the site recovered sheets of gilt copper, both plain and pierced with floral motifs, along with a bronze Chatra (shaft of a tiered). This suggests that the entire spire above the body was once sheathed in gilt copper sheeting.
