Chedi Wat San Nhong

Hot Sub-district, Hot District, Chiang Mai Province

The ancient monument of Wat San Nhong stands on the southern edge of "Thung Chedi" (Chedi Field), an area within the old city of Hot, in Hot Sub-district, Hot District, Chiang Mai Province. The chedi faces east toward the Ping River. No definitive record of the construction survives, but its artistic style and archaeological evidence place it within roughly the 15th to 17th centuries CE.

Past Operations & Excavation
  • 1960 CE (B.E. 2503): Archaeological excavation and site clearance in dam-affected zones by the Fine Arts Department.
  • 1968 CE (B.E. 2511): Archaeological survey of the Hot City area by the 7th Regional Office of Fine Arts, Chiang Mai, and Silpakorn University.
  • 1990 CE (B.E. 2533): Archaeological excavation and clearance project at Wat San Nhong (abandoned site) by the 7th Regional Office of Fine Arts, Chiang Mai.
  • 2021-2026 CE (B.E. 2564-2569): Conservation and development project of Chedi Wat San Nhong by the 7th Regional Office of Fine Arts, Chiang Mai.
Structural Elements

Chedi Wat San Nhong is a bell-shaped chedi in the Lanna art style, set on a square plan measuring 10.2 by 10.2 metres. It stands on a raised platform with brick paving forming a circumambulatory terrace (Pradakshina platform), with access stairs on the southeastern corner.

The Base

The lowest tier is an indented-corner square plinth, adorned with altar platforms on all sides and lotus-bud corner pillars, supporting three receding tiers of indented-corner plinths.

The Body (Relic Chamber)

A large relic chamber on an indented-corner square plan measuring 6.4 metres in width, decorated with bead-and-reel moulding and stucco frames with cloud-like scrollwork in the indented niches.

The Upper Part

Consists of circular-plan stepped plinths in three receding tiers, supporting the bell-shaped body and a square throne. Excavations revealed two diamond-shaped pierced gilt copper sheeting (Thong Jang Ko), presumably used to cover the bell-shaped body. The spire features lotus-petal mouldings with hollow sockets for securing the umbrella shaft (Chatra). Additionally, a western boundary wall extending over 43 metres suggests the former presence of surrounding structures within the precinct.

Artistic Features of Chedi Wat San Nhong

Chedi Ornaments & Gilt Copper Sheeting (Thong Jang Ko): Metal architectural ornaments used to sheath the upper structures including the bell body and spire. Stucco fragments were also recovered, believed to have originally adorned the niche frames on the relic chamber.
